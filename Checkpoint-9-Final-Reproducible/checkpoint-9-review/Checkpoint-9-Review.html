1. Implementation summary

CP9 adds structured comparability constraints, a frozen pretrained semantic encoder and governed case-based historical retrieval. Existing CP1–CP8 logic is preserved. Historical Support for Suitability is secondary operator evidence; it cannot authorise action.

The system uses learned semantic representations to retrieve comparable historical intervention cases, then shows the outcomes and protection trade-offs observed in those cases.

Implemented: immutable point-in-time cases, outcome-aware admission without outcome-based similarity, complete comparable-case descriptors, explicit protection concern, abstention, source inspection, read-only upstream adapters, a separate CP9 SQLite audit store, GET-only service boundaries, A–O scenarios and a portable operator preview.

2. Historical case admission

ClassBehaviour
Comparable, evaluable historyEstablished conforming execution, evaluable Goal finding, observed Protection finding and intact provenance; contributes to the directional denominator when structurally comparable.
Limited historical caseUnestablished/unexecuted/deviated intervention, insufficient outcomes or unresolved conflict; inspectable with limitations, never a clean label.
Rejected from corpusInvalid source envelope, tampered digest, disallowed feature fields or unverifiable version/provenance.

A substantiated Protection breach is adverse evaluable evidence, not an excuse to exclude a case. Admission is separate from query comparability. A valid historical case can still be unsuitable for comparison because critical context is missing or different.

Cases are read from actual saved CP7 decisions and CP8 reviews. The adapters open both source databases read-only. No synthetic “eligible” fixture substitutes for approval.

3. Point-in-time feature representation

featuresFromPlan(plan) accepts only the immutable saved decision-time plan. It does not accept outcome observations or reviews. It explicitly extracts amenity type/tier, pathway/purpose, predeclared contract semantics, scope/function, horizon, protection context, aggregate cohort familiarity/context, change pattern, operations, programme evidence, temporal and resident-mix evidence, and the two sufficiency dimensions.

The historical record separately preserves all original competing-diagnosis assessments, Raw Evidence Support, Relative Evidence Support, coverage, missing evidence and sufficiency. Those fields remain pre-decision records and are not reinterpreted by CP9.

Every evidence source must have been available by the original decision timestamp. The semantic text is the approved scope description, with its approval and scope digest; it uses NFKC normalisation, whitespace collapse and trimming. Resident names, contact data, ownership priorities, outcome summaries and future corrections are not allowed features. Nested field allowlists prevent hidden outcome fields inside otherwise valid objects.

Repair may meet restoration criteria with unchanged usage. Preserve may maintain meaningful access while total use remains low. A pilot can miss its benefit objective while answering its evidence question. Shared-use benefit does not compensate for a breach. There is no universal binary success label.

6. Structured comparability

Comparison occurs before semantic ranking. The conservative first version requires exact, known agreement on amenity type, observability tier, pathway, purpose and purpose ID, contract criteria, affected function and extent, protected access, retained function, observation horizon, decision horizon, dependency/substitute/accessibility context, cohort and reversibility profile.

Temporal, operational and resident-mix comparison records must be available; programme/pilot comparisons also require programme history. Unknown compatibility produces no suitability inference. Cross-type equivalence is not inferred by embeddings. Material dependency or accessibility differences appear as related but non-comparable history.

Change magnitude and other noncritical structured differences remain visible. Exact matching intentionally abstains more often than a future reviewed semantic-compatibility policy might.

7. Hybrid retrieval

For each current CP7 candidate: bind the current query → verify corpus/index digests and as-of validity → retain active non-duplicate records → exclude query amenity/episode/intervention lineage and evaluation family → apply structured comparability → fix membership and feature-only ranking inputs → rank by encoder cosine → attach multidimensional CP8 outcomes → calculate the descriptor using all qualifying evaluable cases.

The structured baseline uses identical hard filters, then orders by noncritical structured differences and stable case ID for exact ties. The hybrid uses semantic ranking within that same admissible set. Neither method changes the historical descriptor denominator.

All qualifying cases contribute even if only three cards are visible per page. The adverse-case summary remains visible across pages.

They are prototype descriptive policy, not statistical adequacy, independence proof, representativeness, calibrated sample size or significance.

9. Protection-aware behaviour

Protection outcomes remain first-class, unweighted findings. Any substantiated comparable breach prevents a supportive descriptor and appears in an always-visible adverse summary. It cannot be discounted by cosine similarity, page position, owner status, cohort size or aggregate benefit.

Case cards show Goal and Protection separately, protected function, accessibility context and reviewed substitute context. Historical concern never mutates current CP7 readiness automatically.

10. CP7 boundary

CP7 candidate eligibility, safeguard gates, both sufficiency assessments, reversibility preference and approval/rationale requirements remain authoritative. Product retrieval is withheld for a candidate that is not Ready. Technical QA can inspect it while retaining its original blocked state.

Stale/conflicting inputs withhold interpretation. The live service re-reads the query after asynchronous embedding and rejects a changed input before persisting a result.

Historical evidence is more supportive for this alternative. CP7 still identifies another adequately supported option as the preferred lower-impact pathway.

Scenario N establishes that tension using an unchanged CP7 comparison with evidenced reversibility profiles, not a CP9 numerical ranking. CP9 has no approval, execution, upstream reassessment, CP4 reranking or CP5 reselection operations.

11. Explainability

Each case exposes matched structured comparison dimensions, the approved historical and query excerpts, important differences, source and approval versions, original diagnosis/sufficiency assessments, CP8 findings, observation qualification and inference limits. The current candidate’s CP7 gates remain inspectable.

Explanations are deterministic records/templates. No generated explanation invents facts. Raw cosine is available only within Technical Details/QA and is labelled retrieval diagnostics, never suitability confidence.

12. Abstention

CP9 explicitly abstains for too little history, no history, material context differences, missing critical comparison fields, unseen combinations, stale/conflicting current inputs and unavailable qualified outcomes. It never chooses an unrelated nearest case or relaxes protection constraints.

If the semantic model is absent or invalid, vectors are not fabricated. A distinct index/audit record identifies Structured comparison only — semantic retrieval unavailable. A model-version mismatch is rejected rather than mixing incompatible vectors. There is no generative fallback.

13. Leakage, versions and duplicates

Similarity features are immutable pre-decision snapshots. Outcomes are separate records. Every result binds query digest, as-of timestamp, corpus release/digest, feature policy/digest, encoder/runtime manifest and index digest.

Only one current review version per intervention episode contributes. Repeated reviews, duplicate records, original corrected findings and superseded reviews remain inspectable without double counting. Contradictory same-version episode payloads reject release creation. A later CP8 review with unresolved independent conflict is limited; a previously favourable review is not substituted for it.

The demo includes a real CP8 same-source authoritative correction: repair-1 changes from Goal Met to Mixed while its original finding remains in history. Case M also includes a repeated review and duplicate version. Query amenity, episode, intervention lineage and held-out generation families are excluded as appropriate.

Corpus release publication is explicit and governed; a new outcome does not trigger automatic retraining or rewrite a previous release.
